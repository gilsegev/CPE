Questions: 
1. ADHD is mainly caused by: 
(correct) a. Low levels of stimulant receptors and brain injuries
b. Lax parenting with few boundaries
c. High-sugar diet
d. Research is still ongoing.

Explanation: We know ADHD is neurodevelopmental and originates in brain function. Lax parenting and diet may contribute to symptom intensity but will not cause ADHD. 


2. Adult diagnosis of ADHD can be tricky because
a. There are very few adult diagnosticians
b. ADHD goes away with age
(correct) c. It is based on self-reporting
d. It is not tricky at all

Explanation: Adult diagnosis relies heavily on self-reporting of symptoms. Many adults develop coping mechanisms and compensate for their ADHD and under-report symptoms. This under-reporting may lead to a wrong diagnosis. 

3. Some common comorbidities of ADHD in children are:
a. Low intelligence
b. Weak skeletal structures
c. Vision and hearing impairments
(correct) d. Dyslexia, dysgraphia, and anxiety

Explanation: A, B, and C all manifest across a bell curve, while dyslexia, dysgraphia, and anxiety are more common for people with ADHD.

4. ADHD is wrongfully considered a ‘boys’ disorder because
a. Most research focuses on girls
b. Most research focuses on boys
c. Boys exhibit more hyperactivity symptoms and are more noticeable
(correct) d. B and C are both correct

Explanation: ADHD Hypoeractive impulsive is more common with boys, making them more noticeable to teachers. They are more diagnosed than girls due to high referral rates. Most research uses more boys in their sample or all-boy samples. There are not enough girl-focused studies.

5. ADHD is highly hereditary
a. (correct) TRUE
b. FALSE

Explanation: ADHD is highly hereditary, with 24-54 genes associated with it. There is also an additive effect; if both parents have ADHD, the child's chances of having it are over 80%.
